Boundary-Layer Turbomachinery: Additive Multi-Disc Tesla Turbine

Engineered, modeled, and 3D-printed a functional boundary-layer Tesla turbine—a bladeless radial-inflow turbomachine operating entirely on fluid viscosity, adhesion, and Prandtl boundary-layer drag rather than traditional aerodynamic blade lift or impulse deflection. Pressurized fluid enters tangentially through a calibrated peripheral accelerator runner, adhering to the smooth faces of a co-axial stack of 7 ultra-thin rotating discs. As the fluid transfers kinetic energy and momentum through viscous shear stress (τ = μ ∂u/∂y), it follows a smooth inward logarithmic spiral toward the low-pressure center, discharging axially through four symmetrical radial vent ports per disc. Supported by dual precision miniature radial ball bearings, the system achieves extreme rotational velocity with zero blade stall, zero cavitation, and superior acoustic dampening.

🌀 Fluid Dynamics & Boundary-Layer Working Principle
  • Prandtl Boundary Layer & Viscous Shear: Eliminates conventional turbine blades, buckets, and vanes. Exploits the no-slip condition at the disc boundary: high-speed air adheres to each disc surface, transferring rotational momentum through pure viscous shear stress without traditional blade deflection losses.
  • Logarithmic Centripetal Spiral: Fluid enters tangentially at the outermost perimeter where peripheral disc velocity is highest. As kinetic energy is imparted to the rotor, fluid velocity decays and smoothly spirals inward toward the central low-pressure exhaust zone.
  • 4-Sector Low-Backpressure Hub Exhaust: Engineered each rotor disc with four symmetrical trapezoidal discharge ports surrounding the central arbor hub. Fluid exhausts axially into matching stator casing ports, preventing aerodynamic choking and high-RPM backpressure build-up.
🔧 Additive Rotor Architecture & Mechanical Tolerancing
  • 7-Disc Co-Axial Rotor Assembly: Constructed a stack of 7 lightweight parallel rotor discs separated by calibrated precision spacing rings. This architecture maximizes total active shear surface area while maintaining an ultra-low moment of inertia for rapid spin-up acceleration.
  • Dual Miniature Radial Ball Bearings: Fitted deep-groove radial ball bearings into precision press-fit seats on both casing halves, isolating radial runout, eliminating axial shaft play, and ensuring near-zero rotational starting friction.
  • Monolithic Tangential Injection Volute: Designed an integrated cylindrical stator housing featuring a dedicated tangential inlet runner that guides compressed air directly along the tangent of the outer disc perimeter at high injection velocity.
  • Additive Skin Friction Optimization: Unlike polished steel discs that struggle to maintain initial boundary layer grip, the micro-textured concentric toolpaths inherent to FDM 3D printing act as microscopic surface ridges that enhance boundary layer adhesion and viscous shear coupling.

1. Four-Bar Linkage Member Lengths Kinematics

Problem & Solution: Initial leg linkages exhibited abrupt, jerky ground strikes and discontinuous momentum. Iteratively tuned the lengths of the four-bar linkage (leg) parts to produce an elongated, fluid ovoid trajectory with smooth foot-plant dynamics.

2. High-Torque Worm-Mesh Gear Drivetrain

Problem & Solution: High chassis mass during single-tripod lift phases caused motor strain and drivetrain play. Increased the size of the gear meshing with the worm gear to significantly increase mechanical torque and decrease backlash.

3. Metal Axle Replacement Structural

Problem & Solution: Plastic axles suffered flexural bending and torsional windup under walking loads, shifting leg phases and placing legs at inconsistent heights. Replaced the plastic axle with a metal axle to eliminate bending and maintain strict, uniform leg synchronization.

4. Underslung Center Battery Dynamics

Problem & Solution: Elevated battery positioning caused roll moments and dynamic tipping during tripod transitions. Moved the battery into the center and on the bottom to level out weight distribution and substantially lower the robot's center of mass.

5. Top-Mounted Inversion Flip Posts DFM / DFA

Problem & Solution: Resting the walker upside-down for adjustments previously stressed and bent the delicate leg linkages. Added standoff posts on the top to act as an integrated workbench stand, making it easy to flip over, inspect gears, and service mechanisms without linkage strain.

Motorized Hexapod Kinematic Walker

Conceived, engineered, and physically constructed an operational six-legged walking robot implementing custom planar 4-bar kinematic linkage geometry. Driven by an onboard electric motor transmitting power through a continuous longitudinal driveshaft, the mechanism synchronizes three dual-sided worm-gear boxes. Through five rigorous mechanical design iterations—tuning 4-bar leg proportions for fluid gait, upsizing worm-mesh gears for high torque, replacing plastic with rigid metal axles, centering battery mass underneath for a low CG, and installing top maintenance flip posts—the robot achieves an alternating tripod gait with zero electronic timing jitter, dynamic stability, and robust bench serviceability.

⚙️ Mechanical Transmission & Drivetrain
  • Continuous Longitudinal Driveshaft: A single central transmission shaft distributes rotational torque across the front, middle, and rear axle stations, mechanically locking all 6 legs in flawless synchronicity without electronic timing drift.
  • Triple High-Torque Worm Drives: 24:1 reduction worm-and-spur stages deliver massive torque multiplication to lift chassis mass during single-tripod support phases while inherently preventing back-driving under gravitational loads.
  • Underslung Low-CG Battery Enclosure: The central battery power pack is suspended beneath the primary chassis beams, lowering the roll center and preventing overturning during dynamic stride transitions.
🔧 Kinematic Linkages & Gait Coordination
  • Planar 4-Bar Kinematic Linkage Optimization: Proportionally calibrated bar lengths convert continuous 360° crank rotation into an ovoid, flat-bottomed walking stride that maximizes ground traction duration and rapid return clearance.
  • Alternating Tripod Stance (180° Anti-Phase): Legs are paired into two complementary triangular triads that alternate between support and swing phases, maintaining static equilibrium throughout the entire gait cycle.
  • Dual Live Physical Demonstrations: Validated through continuous live test runs (32s full gait traversal and 14s stride dynamics), proving non-binding pivot kinematics, backlash absorption, and structural rigidity under load.
🛠️ Engineering Design Process (EDP) & 5 Design Iterations
  • 1. Four-Bar Linkage Length Tuning: Changed the member lengths of the four-bar linkage (legs) parts to eliminate jerky foot strikes and achieve a fluid, continuous walking gait.
  • 2. High-Torque Gear Meshing: Increased the size of the gear meshing with the worm gear to amplify torque multiplication and decrease mechanical backlash.
  • 3. Solid Metal Axle Upgrade: Replaced the plastic axle with a metal axle to eliminate bending deflection and prevent legs from being held at different positions.
  • 4. Centered & Underslung Battery: Moved the battery into the center and on the bottom of the chassis to level out weight and significantly lower the center of mass.
  • 5. Top Inversion Flip Posts: Added vertical standoff posts on the top of the chassis to make the robot easy to flip over, inspect, and work on at the bench without straining leg pivots.

Case Study: Simulated Mun Mission Architecture

Designed and simulated a complete 5-stage spacecraft architecture in Kerbal Space Program (KSP), engineered for orbital insertion, trans-lunar transfer, surface touchdown, and atmospheric reentry recovery. Calculated delta-V requirements using the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation to ensure mission feasibility across all flight regimes.

Vehicle Part Count 147 Parts Optimized for structural integrity & fairing volume
Staging Architecture 5 Discrete Stages Maximizes vacuum specific impulse ($I_{sp}$)
🚀 Section 1: Design Philosophy & Multistage Integration (Page 8)
  • Staging Strategy (Maximizing Vacuum $I_{sp}$): Discarding dead structural mass (empty fuel tanks and spent booster casings) drastically increases the vehicle mass ratio ($m_0 / m_f$). Initial ascent utilizes high sea-level thrust solid and liquid boosters (TWR > 1.8), transitioning to high vacuum specific impulse engines ($I_{sp} > 350\text{s}$) for trans-munar burns.
  • Gravity Turn Optimization: Executed a continuous aerodynamic pitch-over maneuver through Kerbin's 70km atmosphere. Locking vehicle thrust to the surface prograde vector minimizes cosine steering losses and atmospheric drag while converting vertical impulse into horizontal orbital velocity.
🌕 Section 2: Landing Dynamics & Surface Operations (Page 9)
  • Wide-Stance Quad Landing Gear: Low center-of-gravity quad footprint resists tip-over moments on sloped lunar crater walls up to 15° inclination. Telescopic oleo struts with integrated damping absorb touchdown impact without elastic rebound.
  • Deceleration Profile ("Suicide Burn"): Precision throttle governance zeroes out horizontal and vertical velocities simultaneously meters above the surface, eliminating fuel-costly hover phases and minimizing gravity drag.
🪂 Section 3: Ascent & Re-Entry
  • Ascent Staging: Descent stage serves as a stationary launch base; decoupling the upper ascent capsule sheds dead structural mass for the return trip.
  • Parachute Recovery: Blunt heat shield dissipates >3,200 m/s entry velocity, followed by staged triple parachutes slowing the capsule to <6 m/s.
🔬 Systems Evaluation & Takeaway
  • Real-World Aerospace Validation: Demonstrates that real-time physics environments function as valid Digital Twins to verify multi-variable trade-offs in aerospace staging logic before costly hardware fabrication.

Monolithic Compliant Mechanism: Push-Button YES/NO Decision Indexer

Conceived, modeled, and 3D-printed a fully mechanical, pocket-sized decision maker featuring an entirely monolithic compliant flexure suspension and an internal 8-tooth ratchet escapement. Instead of relying on traditional metal coil springs, pins, or fasteners, the return stroke is powered by 3D-printed dual symmetrical serpentine leaf springs integrated directly into the linear push-rod slider. Each press of the tactile plunger translates linear stroke displacement into a discrete 45-degree rotational step (360° / 8 = 45°) via an elastic cantilever driving pawl, toggling the internal dial between YES and NO through a framed aperture in a parametric honeycomb faceplate.

⚙️ Compliant Flexure & Escapement Kinematics
  • Monolithic Serpentine Leaf Springs: Dual symmetrical multi-wave flexures engineered within the elastic strain limits of the thermoplastic filament (PLA/PETG), providing consistent linear restoring force with zero mechanical creep or fatigue settling.
  • Single-Stage Overrunning Escapement: An integrated cantilever pawl arm deflects elastically over the angled ramp of the 8-tooth ratchet during the return stroke, then locks solidly against the steep driving face upon the subsequent forward stroke to advance the disc by exactly 45°.
  • Linear-to-Rotary Conversion: Converts tactile thumb depression into incremental rotary indexing without gears, belts, or external energy sources.
🔧 Additive Tolerancing & EDC Ergonomics
  • Parametric Arbor & Low-Friction Journal: Engineered a central shaft journal with calibrated diametral clearance (0.25mm) ensuring smooth, uninhibited rotation while preventing axial tilt under single-sided pawl actuation.
  • Interchangeable Honeycomb Faceplates: Designed snap-fit hexagonal perforated screens (tested in high-contrast yellow and stealth black) that shield the internal mechanism while framing the embossed typography within a dedicated viewing aperture.
  • Tactile Knurled Bezel: Outer casing features circumferential knurling for secure single-handed grip and actuation.

Kinematic Intermittent Motion: 3-Slot, 4-Slot & 6-Slot Geneva Drives

Conceived, modeled, and 3D-printed three distinct functional Geneva mechanisms (Maltese cross indexing drives) to investigate intermittent rotary motion, duty-cycle timing, and positive dwell locking mechanics across varying slot counts. The portfolio compares a 6-slot drive (60° indexing, 1:2 motion-to-dwell ratio where dwell dominates), a standard 4-slot system (90° indexing, 1:1 symmetric duty cycle), and a geometrically challenging 3-slot system (120° indexing, 2:1 motion-to-dwell ratio). To achieve bind-free operation right off the print bed, each design incorporates tangential pin entry geometry (θ = 90° at engagement) to eliminate impact shock, an integrated circular dwell collar to suppress rotational backlash, and rigid center-distance spacer linkages that precisely fix shaft journal pitch based on theoretical center distance C = R / sin(π/n).

⚙️ Kinematic Geometry & Dwell Mechanics
  • Tangential Pin Entry (θ = 90°): The drive pin enters and exits each slot along a vector exactly perpendicular to the driving crank arm and parallel to the slot centerline, ensuring zero instantaneous relative acceleration at engagement to prevent mechanical shock.
  • Continuous-to-Intermittent Translation: Converts uniform continuous rotary input into regulated, incremental angular steps with positive stationary dwell phases without requiring electronic sensors or clutches.
  • Integrated Convex Locking Arc: The drive wheel integrates a raised circular collar that nests perfectly into the matching concave flank of the Geneva wheel during dwell, mechanically immobilizing the driven wheel against external torque or vibration.
🔧 Additive Tolerancing & Fixturing
  • Rigid Center-Distance Linkage: Engineered a custom two-journal spacer bar maintaining the exact theoretical shaft center distance C = R / sin(π/n), preventing tooth binding or sloppy disengagement.
  • FDM Print Clearance Optimization: Calibrated pin journal diameters (-0.15mm diametral clearance), radial slot width (+0.20mm clearance), and bottom-layer elephant's foot compensation to guarantee effortless hand spinning without post-machining or lubrication.
  • Dwell Clearance Tuning: Applied a 0.25mm radial relief between the convex drive rim and the concave star wheel contour to balance zero-backlash holding torque with minimal sliding friction.

Wind-Powered Kinematic Transport Prototype

Conceived, modeled, and physically built an autonomous wind-propelled rover capable of crawling directly into headwind airflow. Harnessing wind currents with a custom 3D-printed multi-blade turbine impeller, the rover routes rotational kinetic energy through a multi-stage transmission: a lower spur gear reduction to drive the front wheels, an angled universal joint linkage, and a top 90-degree bevel gearbox driving dual crank arms. Validated on a dedicated test bench against an electric fan airflow, proving sustainable forward mechanical propulsion against aerodynamic resistance.

⚙️ Fluid Dynamics & Impeller Synthesis
  • Aerodynamic Pitch Optimization: Iterated through multiple 3D-printed blade angles in TinkerCAD, evaluating 0° flat vs. 45° helical pitch to minimize startup stall speed and extract maximum rotational torque.
  • Multi-Blade Rotor Dynamics: High-solidity blade layout captures low-velocity airflow efficiently, generating sufficient starting torque to overcome static chassis inertia and rolling resistance.
🔧 Mechanical Drivetrain & Kinematics
  • Multi-Stage Gear Reduction: Meshed spur gear stage inside a Technic cage steps up mechanical torque from the high-RPM turbine shaft to the front drive axle.
  • 90° Bevel Gearbox & U-Joint: Angled universal joint transfers rotational power up to a top bevel gear stage driving dual counter-phased crank arms.
  • Kinematic Drag Struts: Trailing support struts provide 3-point static stability, preventing aerodynamic pitch-back under high frontal wind drag.
